### Legacy sign-on service comes back to bite school software provider Bromcom
- Source: theregister.com
- Source type: independent; confidence: high; trust score: 82/100
- Published: Tuesday, October 6, 2026
- Feed: The Register security
- Link: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/06/legacy-sign-on-service-comes-back-to-bite-school-software-provider-bromcom/5301156
- What happened: Bromcom, a UK education software provider, notified customers of a personal data breach affecting its legacy SSO technology. The breach involved unauthorized access to email addresses and limited registration information from the legacy SSO component, which was withdrawn from production after being identified on September 6. The affected component did not hold passwords or authentication tokens, and the incident did not compromise Microsoft or Google accounts. Bromcom serves over 5,000 schools and 390 multi-academy trusts in the UK.
- Why it matters: What question should a seller ask? 'How is your customer managing the lifecycle of legacy SSO components to prevent similar breaches?'
- Technical impact: Unauthorized access to email addresses and limited registration data from legacy SSO component; no password or token exposure.
- Business impact: Potential reputational damage and regulatory scrutiny for UK education sector software provider; disruption to school operations due to service withdrawal.
- Caveat: No evidence of actual data exfiltration or financial loss
- Caveat: No details on forensic findings or remediation timeline
- Evidence-backed points:
  - Bromcom notified customers of a personal data breach affecting its single sign-on (SSO) technology
  - The legacy SSO registration functionality had remained in production after being superseded because 'it was still being called by an internal system'

## AI and identity

### AWS AgentCore security undone by prompt requesting credentials
- Source: theregister.com
- Source type: independent; confidence: high; trust score: 82/100
- Published: Friday, October 9, 2026
- Feed: The Register security
- Link: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/09/aws-agentcore-security-undone-by-prompt-requesting-credentials/5302436
- What happened: The source describes a security vulnerability in AWS AgentCore where an attacker could exploit IMDSv1 credentials to access sensitive AWS resources. The vulnerability was disclosed by Zenity Labs in December 2025 and resolved by AWS by September 2026.
- Why it matters: What question should a seller ask? 'How does your client ensure that agent credentials are not exposed via insecure IMDS endpoints and that IAM roles are scoped to individual agents rather than entire regions?'
- Technical impact: Attackers could extract temporary IAM credentials via IMDSv1, use them to enumerate agents, access container images, and persistently hijack agent sessions through SSRF attacks.
- Business impact: Potential for widespread compromise of AWS agents in a single region due to insufficient network isolation and overprivileged roles.
- Caveat: The source describes a hypothetical scenario (Bob) but claims it was observed by Zenity Labs
- Caveat: AWS claims the vulnerability was not a documented issue, but Zenity reported it as a real problem
- Evidence-backed points:
  - The AgentCore service used IMDSv1 for metadata endpoints, which provided temporary credentials to attackers
  - The default AgentCore IAM role was overprivileged, allowing attackers to access all agents in the region

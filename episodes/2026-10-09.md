### Making native geofencing work for cloud non-human identities
- Source: silverfort.com
- Source type: vendor; confidence: medium; trust score: 69/100
- Published: Thursday, October 8, 2026
- Feed: Silverfort
- Link: https://www.silverfort.com/blog/geofencing-for-cloud-non-human-identities/
- What happened: Non-human identities (NHIs) like service accounts and API keys are the most prevalent identity types in corporate environments. They behave predictably with limited geographic activity compared to humans. Cloud NHIs typically authenticate from a small, stable set of IPs (75% from 25 or fewer addresses, 20% from one address). Identity providers (Entra ID, AWS) natively support IP restrictions but manual policy creation doesn’t scale. Silverfort’s geofencing automation analyzes NHI authentication history to recommend and enforce IP-based policies at scale without manual intervention.
- Why it matters: What’s the most common geographic source for this NHI’s authentication attempts? How does this help us identify when an NHI is being misused?
- Technical impact: By restricting NHIs to their legitimate geographic sources, the risk of lateral movement and unauthorized access is reduced. This is especially critical for AI-powered attackers who exploit the window between detection and response.
- Business impact: Reduces the attack surface for NHIs, which are often the entry point for breaches. This directly impacts operational risk and compliance with regulations requiring strict access controls.
- Caveat: Source is vendor classification (Silverfort), so claims about product capabilities are vendor assertions. Evidence for scale and real-world effectiveness is limited to the 150 customer environments mentioned in the source.
- Caveat: The source does not provide independent validation of the effectiveness of Silverfort’s geofencing automation across all environments.
- Evidence-backed points:
  - Cloud non-human identities (NHIs) authenticate from a small, stable set of IP addresses
  - Identity providers (Entra ID, AWS) natively support IP-based restrictions for NHIs

### Legacy sign-on service comes back to bite school software provider Bromcom
- Source: theregister.com
- Source type: independent; confidence: high; trust score: 82/100
- Published: Tuesday, October 6, 2026
- Feed: The Register security
- Link: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/06/legacy-sign-on-service-comes-back-to-bite-school-software-provider-bromcom/5301156
- What happened: Bromcom, a UK education software provider, notified customers of a personal data breach affecting its legacy SSO technology. The breach involved unauthorized access to email addresses and limited registration information from the legacy SSO component, which was withdrawn from production after being identified on September 6. The affected component did not hold passwords or authentication tokens, and the incident did not compromise Microsoft or Google accounts. Bromcom serves over 5,000 schools and 390 multi-academy trusts in the UK.
- Why it matters: What question should a seller ask? 'How is your customer managing the lifecycle of legacy SSO components to prevent similar breaches?'
- Technical impact: Unauthorized access to email addresses and limited registration data from legacy SSO component; no password or token exposure.
- Business impact: Potential reputational damage and regulatory scrutiny for UK education sector software provider; disruption to school operations due to service withdrawal.
- Caveat: No evidence of actual data exfiltration or financial loss
- Caveat: No details on forensic findings or remediation timeline
- Evidence-backed points:
  - Bromcom notified customers of a personal data breach affecting its single sign-on (SSO) technology
  - The legacy SSO registration functionality had remained in production after being superseded because 'it was still being called by an internal system'
